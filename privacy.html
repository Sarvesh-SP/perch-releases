Privacy, plainly.

Applies to Perch for Mac and Perch for iPhone. Last updated 4 November 2026.

Perch doesn't collect your data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. The developer can't see your tasks, notes or anything else you put in Perch.

Where your data lives

Your tasks, notes, learning plans and focus history (and, if you turn on the Money or Runs tab, your budget and runs) are stored on your device. If you turn on iCloud sync, they're also stored in your private iCloud database, run by Apple, so your devices can share them. Only your Apple ID can read it.

Optional connections, only when you turn them on

  • Calendar — Perch reads today's events to show them. It never changes your calendar.
  • Reminders — Perch keeps a “Perch” list in Apple Reminders in sync with your tasks.
  • Apple Notes — Perch keeps notes in a “Perch” folder in the Notes app in sync.
  • Microphone and speech — only while you hold dictation, to turn speech into a task. Recognition runs on your Mac.
  • Screenshots folder — to show your recent screenshots in the Shots tab.
  • Clipboard history (off by default) — text you copy is kept on your Mac only, never synced, and copies from password managers or marked as secret are skipped.
  • AI agents — Perch runs a server that only accepts connections from your own Mac, protected by a secret token, so tools like Claude Code can read and update your plan when you ask them to.

Things that do reach the internet

  • Update checks — Perch for Mac checks this website for a new version (once a day by default; you can turn it off). Like any website, the host sees your IP address and app version.
  • Link titles — when you paste a web link as a task, Perch fetches that page's title.
  • iCloud — when sync is on, through Apple's iCloud under Apple's privacy policy.

Crash reports and feedback

macOS and iOS give Perch crash and hang reports. Perch keeps them on your device. They're only sent if you choose “Send feedback” and send the email yourself.
